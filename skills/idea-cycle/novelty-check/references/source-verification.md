# Source verification for closest prior work

Apply this to every candidate paper before using it to decide overlap. A failed verification stays visible; it is not silently removed from the candidate list.

## Identity

1. Record title, authors, date/year, venue or preprint status, primary URL, and DOI/arXiv identifier only when actually found. Resolve the identifier against its primary record: arXiv for an arXiv ID, publisher/DOI registration for a DOI. Cross-check scholarly metadata when ambiguity remains.
2. Match title, first author and identifiers. Explain differences between preprint and published years or versions instead of merging them silently. If identifiers fail, search the exact title plus author through another available scholarly source; a fuzzy title match is a lead, not confirmation.
3. Record the lookup outcome in natural language: identity confirmed, unresolved match, temporarily unavailable, or malformed input. State the attempted URL and reason. Do not fabricate titles, IDs, authors or venues from memory.

## Reading and claim support

Record separately **what was retrieved** and **what was actually read**: search snippet; metadata/abstract; selected sections (name them); or full text including the relevant appendix. A successful download is not a full read.

For any decisive overlap or delta:

- Open the primary paper and inspect the mechanism, assumptions, stated result and evidence relevant to the candidate. Read the dependent appendix/supplement where needed.
- Retain a section/page, equation/theorem/table locator and a short quoted passage or faithful paraphrase. Explain which candidate Claim it supports and under which setting.
- A title match verifies neither novelty nor coverage. An abstract may triage a paper, but its silence about a mechanism cannot establish a key difference. Another paper's related-work claim is a pointer back to the original, not a replacement for reading it.
- Preserve conflicting versions and evidence against your preferred judgment. Where a difference rests on information not available in the retrieved text, mark it unknown.

## Missing material

Use accessible, authorized primary copies or existing local materials. Respect access restrictions; a paywall or denied permission is not authorization to bypass it. If full text, a supplement, metadata or a required search source remains unavailable, state exactly how this limits the comparison. Ask for the specific missing material or permission needed, not an open-ended new research project.

Never convert “no hit”, network failure, absent abstract detail or missing reviewer access into “novel”. Equally, preserve supported differences and covered subclaims instead of collapsing every partial limitation into a blanket inconclusive outcome.
